# Legal Agreements for Sponsorships

⚠️ **REQUIRES ATTORNEY CONSULTATION** ⚠️

Template contracts are **NOT sufficient**. Hire an intellectual property attorney to draft these agreements.

## 1. Sponsorship Agreement (All Tiers)

**Applies to:** Basic, Premium, and Enterprise sponsors

**Must include:**

- Monthly fee and payment terms (net 30, credit card, wire transfer)
- Billing start date and recurring billing authorization
- Placement guarantees (specific pages, display priority)
- Term length (month-to-month vs 3/6/12 month contracts)
- Termination clauses:
  - Either party can terminate with 30 days written notice
  - Immediate termination for breach of terms
  - Pro-rated refunds (or no refunds) upon termination
- Content requirements:
  - Sponsor must provide logo, description, URLs
  - Sponsor warrants they have rights to all provided content
  - We reserve right to reject inappropriate content
- Content approval process:
  - How we review sponsored resources before going live
  - Timeline for approval (e.g., 5 business days)
- Reporting obligations:
  - Monthly click reports provided by [date] each month
  - What metrics are included (clicks by location, by resource)
  - What metrics are NOT included (no PII, no conversion data)
- Limitation of liability:
  - Cap on damages (e.g., 12 months of fees paid)
  - No consequential damages
- Representations and warranties:
  - Sponsor warrants products are legal, safe, appropriate for children
  - Sponsor warrants no trademark/copyright infringement

**Attorney to draft:** Full contract (not template)

## 2. Non-Disclosure Agreement (Enterprise Only)

**Applies to:** Enterprise tier sponsors only (those receiving PR write access)

**Must include:**

- Definition of confidential information:
  - Source code and technical documentation
  - Business information (revenue, user metrics, roadmap)
  - Trade secrets and proprietary methods
- Mutual obligations (protects both parties):
  - We don't disclose their business info
  - They don't disclose our source code
- Permitted disclosures:
  - Required by law (with notice if possible)
  - Already publicly known
  - Independently developed
- Duration:
  - During sponsorship term + 2 years after termination
- Return of materials:
  - Upon termination, sponsor must delete/destroy all confidential info
  - Remove access to private GitHub repository
- Remedies for breach:
  - Injunctive relief (immediate court order to stop disclosure)
  - Monetary damages

**Attorney to draft:** Mutual NDA customized for software

## 3. Non-Compete Agreement (Enterprise Only)

**Applies to:** Enterprise tier sponsors only

**Must include:**

- Prohibited activities:
  - Cannot build competing homeschool management software
  - Cannot hire our employees/contractors to build competitor
  - Cannot use our confidential info to benefit competitor
- Reasonable scope (must be enforceable):
  - **Geography**: Ideally worldwide (software has no borders)
  - **Time**: During sponsorship + 12 months after termination
  - **Industry**: Homeschool management software for Pre-K to 12th grade
- Permitted activities:
  - Sponsor can continue their existing products
  - Sponsor can offer general educational content
  - Sponsor can partner with other apps that aren't direct competitors
- Severability clause:
  - If any provision is unenforceable, rest of agreement stays valid
  - Court can modify overly broad provisions to make them enforceable
- Jurisdiction:
  - Which state's law applies (your state)
  - Where disputes are resolved (arbitration or specific court)

**Attorney considerations:**

- Non-competes are **NOT enforceable in California**
- Check your state's requirements for enforceable non-competes
- Overly broad agreements are thrown out entirely in some states
- Must have "consideration" (the sponsorship fee is the consideration)

**Attorney to draft:** Non-compete customized for your state

## 4. Contributor License Agreement (Enterprise Only)

**Applies to:** Enterprise tier sponsors who submit PRs to private repo

**Must include:**

- Grant of copyright license:
  - Sponsor grants us perpetual, irrevocable license to their contributed code
  - We can use, modify, distribute, sublicense their contributions
  - We retain ownership of overall codebase
- Grant of patent license:
  - If sponsor has patents covering their contribution, they license them to us
  - Prevents sponsor from later suing us for patent infringement
- Representations:
  - Sponsor warrants they own/control the code they submit
  - Code doesn't infringe anyone else's IP
  - Code is not subject to conflicting licenses (e.g., GPL)
- Original work:
  - Contributions are sponsor's original work OR properly licensed
  - If using third-party code, it's compatible with our proprietary license
- No obligation to accept:
  - We're not required to merge/use their PRs
  - We can modify or reject contributions
- Attribution:
  - How sponsor is credited (git commit author, CONTRIBUTORS file)
- Moral rights waiver:
  - Sponsor waives "moral rights" (right to be identified as author in some jurisdictions)

**Model CLAs to reference (but customize):**

- Apache CLA: https://www.apache.org/licenses/contributor-agreements.html
- Google CLA: https://cla.developers.google.com/

**Attorney to draft:** CLA customized for proprietary codebase

## 5. Additional Considerations

### Invoice/Payment Terms

- Create invoice template with:
  - Payment due date (net 30)
  - Accepted payment methods
  - Late payment penalties (1.5% per month, or as allowed by state law)
  - Contact for billing questions

### Privacy Policy Update

- Add section about sponsored content:
  - What we track (anonymous clicks)
  - What we don't track (student PII)
  - How sponsors use click data (aggregate reporting only)
  - User control (settings toggle to hide sponsored content)
- Must comply with COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act)
  - No collection of PII from children under 13
  - Our anonymous click tracking should be compliant, but verify

### Terms of Service Update

- Add section about third-party links:
  - We're not responsible for sponsor websites
  - User clicks external links at their own risk
  - We don't endorse sponsor products (just provide placement)

**Worth it?** Yes, because:

- One bad contract could cost you tens of thousands in litigation

- Weak CLA = sponsor claims ownership of your code
- COPPA violation = FTC fines up to $50,000 per violation

## Next Steps

1. **Find an IP attorney**:
   - Specializes in software/SaaS contracts
   - Familiar with open-source licensing (for attribution requirements)
   - Licensed in your state (for non-compete enforceability)

2. **Schedule initial consultation** (usually 30-60 min, sometimes free):
   - Explain business model (sponsorships for homeschool app)
   - Ask about timeline and costs
   - Request flat fee quote if possible (instead of hourly)

3. **Provide attorney with**:
   - This requirements document
   - Copy of your software (demo or screenshots)
   - Competitor analysis (who else is in the space)
   - Target sponsor list (IXL, Sonlight, etc.)

4. **Review drafts carefully**:
   - Understand every clause before signing with sponsors
   - Ask attorney to explain anything unclear
   - Request "business-friendly" language (not overly legal jargon)

5. **Budget timeline**:
   - Initial consultation: Week 1
   - Attorney drafts agreements: Week 2-4
   - Your review and revisions: Week 5
   - Final agreements ready: Week 6

**Do NOT sign sponsors until agreements are ready!**
